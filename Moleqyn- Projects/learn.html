Nature & States of Matter
Solid, Liquid, Gas — Particle arrangement, properties & phase transitions
Class 6Easy
▶

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. It is composed of tiny particles that attract one another and are in constant movement.

Syllabus Subtopics (NCERT/ICSE)

  • Three fundamental states: Solids (definite shape & volume), Liquids (definite volume, indefinite shape), Gases (indefinite shape & volume)
  • Particle behavior: Intermolecular spaces and intermolecular forces of attraction
  • Phase changes: Melting, Freezing, Boiling, Evaporation, Condensation, and Sublimation
  • Effect of heat on particles — Kinetic energy increases as temperature rises
Key Principle

Intermolecular Force: Solid > Liquid > Gas | Intermolecular Space: Gas > Liquid > Solid

Heat + Solid → Liquid (Melting)Heat + Liquid → Gas (Vaporisation)

Scientific grouping and classification of objects based on observable chemical and physical properties.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Solubility in water: Soluble substances (sugar, salt) vs Insoluble substances (sand, chalk powder)

Why Sort Materials?

Sorting helps in systematic study of properties and observing patterns in material behavior.

Air & Atmospheric Chemistry
Composition of air, Oxygen supporting combustion, Nitrogen, CO₂ & Water vapour
Class 6Easy
▶

Air is a colorless, odorless mixture of gases that surrounds our planet, forming Earth's atmosphere.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Percentage composition of air: Nitrogen (~78%), Oxygen (~21%), Carbon dioxide (~0.04%), Argon and other inert gases, Water vapour, Smoke and Dust
  • Role of Oxygen: Crucial for cellular respiration in living organisms and combustion (burning) of fuels
  • Role of Nitrogen: Dilutes atmospheric oxygen, regulates combustion rate, essential nutrient builder for plants
  • Carbon dioxide: Produced during respiration and burning; consumed by green plants during photosynthesis
  • Water vapour in air: Determines humidity; triggers precipitation and condensation in clouds
Chemical Fact

Burning requires Oxygen | Smothering a flame with a glass tumbler removes O₂, extinguishing combustion.

N₂: 78%O₂: 21%CO₂ + Noble gases: ~1%

Physical & Chemical Changes
Reversibility, Chemical reactions, Rusting of iron, Crystallisation & Energy changes
Class 7Easy
▶

Classification of transformations in matter based on molecular composition changes and energy exchange.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Physical Changes: Reversible change; no new substance formed; alterations in physical state, size, or shape (e.g., melting ice, stretching rubber)
  • Chemical Changes: Irreversible transformation; new chemical substances with distinct properties are created (e.g., burning magnesium ribbon, curdling of milk)
  • Evidence of Chemical Change: Evolution of gas, change in color, emission of heat/light, change in odor, formation of precipitate
  • Rusting of Iron: Reaction of iron with oxygen and water: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (Hydrated ferric oxide)
  • Prevention of Rusting: Galvanisation (zinc coating), painting, greasing, chrome plating, stainless steel alloying
  • Crystallisation: A physical purification technique to yield pure copper sulphate crystals from impure solution
Classic Lab Reaction

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (white ash) | MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ (Magnesium hydroxide turns red litmus blue)

Fe + CuSO₄ (blue) → FeSO₄ (green) + Cu (red)

Acids, Bases & Salts
Mineral vs organic acids, Natural indicators (Litmus, Turmeric, China Rose) & Neutralisation
Class 7Easy
▶

Study of chemical substances categorized by their sour or bitter taste, ionic nature, and indicator color changes.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Acids: Sour in taste, turn blue litmus paper red. Organic acids (Acetic acid in vinegar, Citric acid in lemon, Lactic acid in curd, Tartaric acid in tamarind, Formic acid in ant sting) vs Mineral acids (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃)
  • Bases & Alkalis: Bitter in taste, soapy to touch, turn red litmus blue. Examples: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH), Calcium hydroxide (lime water), Ammonium hydroxide
  • Indicators: Natural substances showing distinct colors in acidic/basic media (Litmus from lichens, Turmeric turning reddish-brown in base, China rose petal indicator turning dark pink/magenta in acid and green in base)
  • Synthetic indicator: Phenolphthalein (Colorless in acid, bright pink in basic medium)
  • Neutralisation reaction: Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat (Exothermic reaction)
  • Neutralisation in daily life: Antacids for indigestion (Milk of Magnesia), Calamine lotion (Zinc carbonate) for ant stings, Agricultural soil treatment with slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂), Factory waste treatment before disposal
Neutralisation Formula

HCl (Hydrochloric acid) + NaOH (Sodium hydroxide) → NaCl (Sodium chloride) + H₂O + Heat

The World of Metals & Non-metals (Class 7)
New Curiosity Syllabus Ch 4 / ICSE — Physical properties, malleability, ductility & reactivity intro
Class 7Easy
▶

Foundation of chemical elements into metals and non-metals based on observable metallic luster, sonority, and electrical conductivities.

Syllabus Subtopics (Curiosity Textbook)

  • Physical properties of Metals: Malleability (beaten into thin sheets, e.g., gold, aluminium foil), Ductility (drawn into wires, e.g., copper), Sonority (ringing sound), Luster (shine), High thermal & electrical conductivity
  • Physical properties of Non-metals: Brittle, dull appearance, non-sonorous, poor conductors of heat and electricity (insulators)
  • Notable exceptions: Mercury (liquid metal at room temp), Graphite (non-metal that conducts electricity), Iodine (lustrous non-metal), Diamond (hardest non-metal with high thermal conductivity)
  • Reaction with air and oxygen: Formation of basic metal oxides (e.g., Magnesium oxide) vs acidic non-metal oxides (e.g., Sulphur dioxide)
  • Everyday uses of metals (wiring, utensils, construction) and non-metals (oxygen for life, chlorine for water purification, nitrogen in fertilisers)
Crucial Rule of Oxides

Metal Oxides are BASIC (turn red litmus blue) | Non-metal Oxides are ACIDIC (turn blue litmus red)

Elements, Compounds & Mixtures
Curiosity Ch 8 / NCERT — Purity, chemical synthesis, fractional distillation & chromatography
Class 8Medium
▶

Rigorous distinction between chemically bonded compounds and physically combined mixtures.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Element: Pure substance made of one type of atom (Metals, Non-metals, Metalloids, Noble gases)
  • Compound: Pure substance made of two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportion by mass (e.g., H₂O is always 1:8 hydrogen to oxygen by mass)
  • Differences: Mixtures retain constituent properties and can be separated physically; Compounds display entirely new chemical properties and require chemical methods to break down
  • Advanced Separation techniques: Fractional distillation (separation based on boiling point differences), Paper chromatography (separation by adsorption rate), Separating funnel for immiscible liquids
Classic Comparison

Fe + S powder = Mixture (Fe separated by magnet) | Fe + S heated = FeS compound (Non-magnetic, reacts with HCl to yield toxic H₂S gas)

Chemistry of hydrocarbons stored in ancient organic matter and industrial processing of fossil resources.

Combustion, Flame & Fuels
Ignition temperature, Fire triangle, Flame structure zones & Calorific value
Class 8Easy
▶

Thermodynamic study of rapid oxidation reactions producing heat, light, and characteristic flame structures.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Combustion: Exothermic chemical reaction with oxygen releasing thermal and luminous energy
  • Combustible vs Non-combustible substances
  • Conditions necessary for combustion: Presence of combustible substance, Supply of oxygen (supporter of combustion), Attainment of Ignition Temperature
  • Types of combustion: Rapid combustion (gas stove burner), Spontaneous combustion (White phosphorus at room temp), Explosion (fireworks)
  • Structure of a candle flame: Dark innermost zone (unburnt wax vapors), Luminous middle zone (incomplete combustion, yellow, carbon particles), Non-luminous outermost zone (complete combustion, blue, hottest zone)
  • Calorific value of fuel (kJ/kg): Energy released on complete combustion of 1 kg fuel; Ideal fuel properties

Chemical Effects of Electric Current
Electrolytes, Ions, Electrolysis of water, Electroplating & Faraday intro
Class 8Medium
▶

Electrical conductance through liquids leading to chemical decomposition and material deposition.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Conductors vs Insulators in liquids: Pure distilled water is poor conductor; addition of acids, bases, or salts provides mobile ions making it electrolytic
  • Electrolysis: Chemical decomposition of an electrolyte caused by passing direct electric current
  • Electrolysis of acidified water: Hydrogen gas collected at Cathode (-), Oxygen gas collected at Anode (+) in 2:1 volume ratio: 2H₂O → 2H₂↑ + O₂↑

Matter in Our Surroundings
CBSE Ch 1 / ICSE — Kinetic molecular theory, Latent heat of fusion & vaporisation, Evaporation
Class 9Medium
▶

Kinetic Molecular Theory explaining states of matter, thermal phase transitions, and intermolecular forces.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Characteristics of particles of matter: Negligible particle size, continuous random motion (Brownian motion), intermolecular spaces, intermolecular attractions
  • Temperature scales: Celsius (°C) and Kelvin (K): K = °C + 273.15
  • Latent Heat: Hidden heat that changes state without temperature change (Latent heat of fusion = 3.34×10⁵ J/kg for ice; Latent heat of vaporisation = 22.6×10⁵ J/kg for steam)
  • Sublimation and Deposition: Direct Solid ⇌ Gas transitions without liquid phase (Ammonium chloride, Iodine, Camphor, Dry ice / solid CO₂)
  • Evaporation vs Boiling: Surface phenomenon below boiling point vs Bulk phenomenon at constant boiling point
  • Factors affecting evaporation: Surface area (↑), Temperature (↑), Humidity (↓), Wind speed (↑); Cooling effect produced during evaporation
  • Plasma & Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC): 4th and 5th states of matter
Thermal Constant

Latent Heat of Vaporisation of steam produces more severe burns than boiling water at 100°C because of extra stored latent heat.

Is Matter Around Us Pure?
CBSE Ch 2 / ICSE — True solutions, Colloids, Suspensions, Tyndall Effect & Separation
Class 9Medium
▶

Chemical classification into pure elements, compounds, and heterogeneous systems based on particle size and scattering of light.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • True Solution: Homogeneous mixture; particle size <1 nm; stable; passes filter paper; does not scatter light (No Tyndall effect)
  • Colloidal Solution: Heterogeneous mixture; particle size 1–100 nm; translucent; stable; shows Tyndall effect (light beam scattering) and Brownian motion
  • Components of colloid: Dispersed phase and Dispersion medium (Sols, Gels, Emulsions, Aerosols, Foams)
  • Suspension: Heterogeneous mixture; particle size >100 nm; opaque; particles settle under gravity on standing
  • Concentration of solution: Mass % = (Mass of Solute / Mass of Solution) × 100; Volume % = (Vol of Solute / Vol of Solution) × 100
  • Separation of components: Centrifugation (blood/milk testing), Chromatography (dye separation, Rf factor), Fractional distillation
The Tyndall Effect

Scattering of visible light beam by colloidal particles makes the path of light visible (e.g., sunlight through forest canopy or milk in water).

Atoms & Molecules & Mole Concept
CBSE Ch 3 / ICSE — Laws of Chemical Combination, Dalton's theory, Atomic mass & Avogadro's Mole
Class 9Hard
▶

Quantitative chemistry foundation: Mass conservation, constant proportions, and mole counting calculations.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier): Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products
  • Law of Definite / Constant Proportions (Proust): Chemical compound always contains elements in fixed mass ratio
  • Atomic Mass Unit (u / unified amu): Defined as 1/12th the mass of an unbonded Carbon-12 atom
  • Molecules of elements (O₂, N₂, P₄, S₈) vs Molecules of compounds (H₂O, NH₃, CH₄)
  • Molecular Mass & Formula Unit Mass calculations
  • The Mole Concept: 1 mole = 6.02214076 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro Constant NA)
  • Molar Mass: Mass of 1 mole of a substance expressed in grams (g/mol)
  • Mole calculations: Number of moles n = Given Mass (m) / Molar Mass (M) = Number of Particles (N) / NA
Core Calculations

n = m / M | N = n × 6.022×10²³ | 1 mole gas at STP occupies 22.4 L (Standard Temperature & Pressure)

H₂O = 18 g/molCO₂ = 44 g/molCaCO₃ = 100 g/mol

Structure of the Atom
CBSE Ch 4 / ICSE — Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr's models, Subatomic particles & Isotopes
Class 9Hard
▶

Discovery of the subatomic architecture: Protons, Neutrons, Electrons, Nuclear scattering, and Shell energy levels.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Cathode Ray tube experiment (J.J. Thomson discovered electron; Plum Pudding model)
  • Canal Ray experiment (E. Goldstein discovered proton)
  • Chadwick discovered Neutron (1932): Neutral subatomic particle in nucleus with mass ~1 u
  • Rutherford's Alpha Particle Scattering Experiment: Discovery of dense, positively charged atomic nucleus; Mostly empty space
  • Bohr's Model of the Atom: Discrete circular orbits/shells (K, L, M, N); Electrons do not radiate energy while in permitted shells
  • Distribution of electrons (Bohr-Bury scheme): 2n² maximum capacity per shell (K=2, L=8, M=18, N=32); Outermost shell max 8 electrons
  • Atomic Number (Z = Number of Protons) and Mass Number (A = Protons + Neutrons)
  • Valence electrons and Valency determination for elements Z=1 to 20
  • Isotopes: Same Z, different A (Protium ¹H, Deuterium ²H, Tritium ³H; C-12, C-14; Cl-35 & Cl-37 in 3:1 ratio giving average mass 35.5 u); Isobars: Same A, different Z (e.g., ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca)
Bohr-Bury Rule

Maximum electrons in shell n = 2n² | Octet Rule: Maximum 8 electrons in outermost valence shell.

The Language of Chemistry & Gas Laws (ICSE)
ICSE Class 9 Ch 1, 5, 7 — Chemical symbols, Valency, Balancing redox, Boyle's & Charles' Laws
Class 9Hard
▶

Core ICSE curriculum: Chemical nomenclature, radicals, writing balanced stoichiometric equations, and quantitative gas laws.

Syllabus Subtopics (ICSE Special)

  • Chemical formula construction using electrovalent and covalent valencies
  • Variable valency metals: Iron (Fe²⁺ ferrous, Fe³⁺ ferric), Copper (Cu⁺ cuprous, Cu²⁺ cupric)
  • Balancing chemical equations by Hit-and-Trial and algebraic coefficient methods
  • Boyle's Law: Pressure is inversely proportional to Volume at constant temperature (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂)

Combined Gas Law

(P₁ × V₁) / T₁ = (P₂ × V₂) / T₂ (Temperature MUST always be converted to Kelvin)

Chemical Reactions & Equations
CBSE Ch 1 / ICSE — Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Redox, Rancidity & Corrosion
Class 10Medium
▶

Classification and stoichiometric balancing of chemical reactions based on thermodynamics and electron transfer.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Writing and balancing chemical equations (Conservation of atoms)
  • Combination reactions: CaO (quicklime) + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (slaked lime) + Heat (Exothermic)
  • Decomposition reactions: Thermal (2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃; CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂), Electrolytic (2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂), Photochemical (2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ in sunlight used in black & white photography)
  • Displacement reactions: More reactive metal replaces less reactive metal (Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu)

  • Oxidising and Reducing agents identification in live equations
  • Corrosion (rusting of iron, green patina on copper CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂, black coating on silver Ag₂S) and Rancidity of fats/oils
Classic Redox Reaction

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O (CuO is reduced to Cu; H₂ is oxidised to H₂O; CuO is the oxidising agent)

Acids, Bases & Salts (Class 10)
CBSE Ch 2 / ICSE — pH scale, H⁺/OH⁻ ions, Chlor-alkali process, Bleaching powder, Plaster of Paris
Class 10Hard
▶

Arrhenius concept of acids and bases, mathematical pH logarithmic scale, and industrial salt chemistry.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Chemical properties: Acid + Metal → Salt + H₂↑; Acid + Metal Carbonate → Salt + CO₂↑ + H₂O; Acid + Metal Oxide → Salt + H₂O
  • What all acids share in common: Generation of hydronium ions H₃O⁺ in aqueous solution; Pure dry HCl does not turn dry litmus red
  • pH Scale: Quantitative measure of hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]; Acidic (<7), Neutral (=7), Basic (>7)
  • Importance of pH in daily life: Digestive system (HCl in stomach), Tooth decay (starts below pH 5.5), Self-defense by bee stings (Formic acid), Acid rain (pH <5.6)
  • Chlor-alkali Process: Electrolysis of brine (NaCl solution) producing NaOH (caustic soda), Cl₂ gas at anode, H₂ gas at cathode
  • Bleaching Powder: Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O (Used as disinfectant and oxidizing agent)
  • Baking Soda: NaHCO₃ (Sodium hydrogen carbonate); Thermal decomposition yields CO₂ causing cakes to rise; Ingredient of antacids
  • Washing Soda: Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (Sodium carbonate decahydrate); Water softening agent
  • Plaster of Paris: CaSO₄·½H₂O (Calcium sulphate hemihydrate) prepared by heating Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) to 373 K
Key Reactions to Memorize

2NaHCO₃ + Heat → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂↑ | CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O (Sets hard as Gypsum)

Metals & Non-metals & Metallurgy
CBSE Ch 3 / ICSE Ch 7 — Reactivity series, Ionic bond, Extraction of metals, Roasting, Calcination & Refining
Class 10Hard
▶

Chemical reactivity of metals, formation of ionic compounds, and chemical metallurgy from ores to pure metals.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au
  • Reaction with water and acids: K and Na react violently with cold water producing H₂ which catches fire; Mg reacts with hot water; Al, Fe, Zn react only with steam; Cu, Ag, Au do not react
  • Amphoteric oxides: Oxides that react with both acids and bases (Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O; Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O)
  • Formation and properties of Ionic Compounds (e.g., NaCl, MgCl₂): Electron transfer, high melting/boiling points, electrical conductance in molten/aqueous states due to mobile ions
  • Metallurgy & Extraction: Concentration of ore; Roasting (heating sulphide ores in excess air: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂) vs Calcination (heating carbonate ores in limited air: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂)
  • Thermite reaction: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe(l) + Al₂O₃ + Heat (Used to weld railway tracks)
  • Electrolytic refining of copper; Anode mud; Alloying (Brass: Cu+Zn, Bronze: Cu+Sn, Solder: Pb+Sn, Stainless steel: Fe+Cr+Ni)

Carbon & Its Compounds (Organic Chem Intro)
CBSE Ch 4 / ICSE — Covalent bonding, Allotropes, Homologous series, IUPAC, Ethanol, Ethanoic acid & Soaps
Class 10Hard
▶

Versatility of carbon tetravalency, catenation, hydrocarbon families, functional group chemistry, and detergents.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Covalent bonding: Sharing of electron pairs (Single bond in H₂, Double bond in O₂, Triple bond in N₂)
  • Versatile nature of carbon: Catenation (ability to form long chains and rings) and Tetravalency (4 valence electrons)
  • Allotropes of carbon: Diamond (rigid 3D network, sp³), Graphite (hexagonal layers held by weak van der Waals forces, sp², conducts electricity), Fullerenes (C₆₀ buckyball)

  • IUPAC Nomenclature for 1 to 5 carbon compounds

  • Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) & Ethanoic Acid (CH₃COOH / Acetic acid): Esterification reaction (CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O with fruity smell), Saponification
  • Soaps and Detergents: Structure of soap molecule (hydrophilic ionic head + hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail); Mechanism of micelle formation and cleansing action in hard vs soft water

Acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester (fragrant) | Ester + NaOH → Soap (Sodium salt of fatty acid) + Alcohol (Saponification)

ICSE Class 10 Ch 8 — Fountain experiment, Haber process, Ostwald process & Contact process

  • SN1 Mechanism (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular): Two steps via Carbocation intermediate; Rate = k[R-X]; Order of reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1°; Causes Racemisation
  • SN2 Mechanism (Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular): Single concerted step via Pentacoordinated Transition State; Rate = k[R-X][Nu⁻]; Order of reactivity: CH₃X > 1° > 2° > 3° (steric hindrance); Inversion of configuration (Walden Inversion)
  • Elimination reactions: Saytzeff's Rule (Dehydrohalogenation yields more highly substituted, stable alkene as major product)
  • Reactivity of Haloarenes: Extremely unreactive towards nucleophilic substitution due to resonance stabilization (partial double bond C-Cl character), sp² hybridised carbon, and repulsion by aromatic π-cloud
  • Electrophilic substitution in chlorobenzene: ortho-para directing due to +R effect overcoming -I effect
  • Sandmeyer reaction: Aniline + NaNO₂/HCl at 0–5°C → Benzene diazonium chloride; Reaction with Cu₂Cl₂/HCl yields Chlorobenzene
  • Polyhalogen compounds: Dichloromethane, Chloroform (CHCl₃ stored in dark bottles to prevent phosgene COCl₂ poison gas formation), Iodoform test, Freons (CFCs), DDT
SN1 vs SN2 Comparison

SN1: Polar protic solvent | 2 steps via 3° carbocation | Racemisation | Rate ∝ [Substrate]
SN2: Polar aprotic solvent | 1 step back-side attack on 1° halide | Complete Inversion | Rate ∝ [Substrate][Nucleophile]

Oxygen-containing functional group chemistry: Acidity of phenols, electrophilic substitution of phenoxide, and ether cleavage mechanisms.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Alcohols: Preparation by hydration of alkenes, Hydroboration-Oxidation (anti-Markovnikov hydration), Reduction of aldehydes/ketones/esters, Grignard reagents (RMgX) with aldehydes/ketones
  • Physical properties: High boiling points due to intermolecular hydrogen bonding; Solubility decreases as alkyl chain size increases
  • Chemical properties: Cleavage of O-H bond (acidity: 1° > 2° > 3° alcohols; Esterification); Cleavage of C-O bond (reaction with HX, Lucas Test: conc. HCl + anhyd. ZnCl₂ distinguishes 3° immediate turbidity, 2° 5 mins, 1° no turbidity at room temp)
  • Dehydration of alcohols to alkenes: Acid-catalysed mechanism via carbocation; Oxidation (1° → aldehyde with PCC; 1° → carboxylic acid with CrO₃/KMnO₄; 2° → ketone)
  • Phenols: Acidic nature (Phenol is more acidic than alcohol because phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance); Kolbe's Reaction (Phenol + NaOH + CO₂ → Salicylic acid → Aspirin); Reimer-Tiemann Reaction (Phenol + CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH → Salicylaldehyde); Reaction with Zinc dust (Phenol + Zn → Benzene)
  • Ethers: Preparation by Williamson Ether Synthesis: R-ONa + R'-X → R-O-R' + NaX (R'-X MUST be primary halide to prevent elimination); Cleavage of C-O bond by HI (Cleaves to alcohol and iodide via SN2 for 1°/2°, but via SN1 yielding 3° alkyl iodide for tertiary alkyl groups)
Named Reactions of Phenol

Kolbe's: Phenoxide + CO₂ (400 K, 4–7 atm) → Salicylic Acid (used to make Aspirin)
Reimer-Tiemann: Phenol + CHCl₃ + 3NaOH → Salicylaldehyde (ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde)

Aldehydes, Ketones & Carboxylic Acids
CBSE/ISC Unit 8 — Nucleophilic addition, Aldol condensation, Cannizzaro, Tollens' & Fehling's tests, HVZ reaction
Class 12Hard
▶

Carbonyl chemistry: Nucleophilic additions across polarized C=O, enolate condensations, and carboxylic acid derivatives.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Carbonyl structure: Planar sp² hybridised carbon with polar C=O dipole
  • Preparation: Rosenmund reduction (RCOCl + H₂ / Pd-BaSO₄ → RCHO), Stephen reaction (RCN + SnCl₂/HCl → RCHO), Etard reaction (Toluene + CrO₂Cl₂ → Benzaldehyde), Ozonolysis, Hydration of alkynes
  • Nucleophilic Addition Reactions: Addition of HCN (cyanohydrins), NaHSO₃, Grignard reagents, Alcohols (acetals/ketals), Ammonia derivatives (oximes, hydrazones, 2,4-DNP derivatives)
  • Reactivity order: HCHO > RCHO > RCOR (aldehydes more reactive than ketones due to steric and inductive effects)
  • Distinguishing Tests: Tollens' Test (ammoniacal AgNO₃ forms Silver Mirror with aldehydes), Fehling's Test (forms red Cu₂O precipitate with aliphatic aldehydes), Iodoform Test (compounds with CH₃-C=O or CH₃-CH(OH)- form yellow CHI₃ precipitate with I₂ + NaOH)
  • Aldol Condensation: Aldehydes/ketones having α-hydrogen react with dilute alkali to yield β-hydroxy aldehydes, which on heating lose water to form α,β-unsaturated carbonyls; Cross-Aldol
  • Cannizzaro Reaction: Aldehydes lacking α-hydrogen (HCHO, C₆H₅CHO) undergo self redox with conc. KOH: 2HCHO + KOH → CH₃OH + HCOOK
  • Carboxylic Acids: Acidic strength (electron-withdrawing groups -NO₂, -Cl increase acidity; electron-donating groups decrease acidity; Formic acid > Benzoic acid > Acetic acid); Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction: R-CH₂-COOH + Cl₂/Red P → R-CH(Cl)-COOH
Aldol vs Cannizzaro Rule

Has α-Hydrogen? → Undergoes ALDOL CONDENSATION (e.g., Acetaldehyde, Acetone)
NO α-Hydrogen? → Undergoes CANNIZZARO REACTION (e.g., Formaldehyde, Benzaldehyde)

Organic nitrogen bases, amine nucleophilicity, synthetic versatility of arenediazonium salts, and azo dye synthesis.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Classification: 1°, 2°, 3° amines and quaternary ammonium salts
  • Preparations: Reduction of nitro compounds (R-NO₂ + Fe/HCl → R-NH₂), Ammonolysis of alkyl halides, Reduction of nitriles/amides with LiAlH₄

  • Carbylamine Test: Primary amine (aliphatic or aromatic) + CHCl₃ + 3KOH + Heat → Isocyanide (R-NC) with extremely foul, offensive odor (Specific test for 1° amines)
  • Hinsberg's Test: Benzenesulphonyl chloride distinguishes 1° (soluble in alkali), 2° (insoluble in alkali), and 3° amines (no reaction)
  • Diazonium Salts (Ar-N₂⁺ Cl⁻): Prepared by diazotisation of aniline with NaNO₂ + HCl at 273–278 K; Synthetic utility: Sandmeyer reaction (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN), Gattermann reaction, Balz-Schiemann reaction (fluorination), Azo coupling reactions with phenol (orange dye) and β-naphthol (scarlet dye)

Carbylamine: R-NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3alc. KOH → R-NC (foul smelling isocyanide)

Biomolecules (Biochemistry)

▶

Molecular chemistry of biological macromolecules: Stereochemistry of carbohydrates, amino acid zwitterions, protein structures, and nucleic acids.

Syllabus Subtopics

  • Carbohydrates: Classification (Monosaccharides, Oligosaccharides, Polysaccharides; Reducing sugars with free aldehyde/ketone vs Non-reducing e.g., Sucrose)
  • Glucose (Aldohexose): Open-chain structure proofs (HI gives n-hexane, NH₂OH gives oxime, Br₂-water gives Gluconic acid, conc. HNO₃ gives Saccharic acid); Cyclic hemiacetal structure (α and β anomers differing at C1 anomeric carbon); Mutarotation; Haworth projections; Fructose (Ketohexose)

  • Polysaccharides: Starch (Amylose water-soluble unbranched + Amylopectin branched), Cellulose (unbranched β-D-glucose polymer with structural support in plant cell walls), Glycogen (animal starch)
  • Amino Acids: α-Amino carboxylic acids; Zwitterion structure (dipolar ion with neutral net charge at Isoelectric Point pI); Essential (must be taken in diet, e.g., Valine, Leucine) vs Non-essential amino acids

  • Enzymes: Biocatalysts, lock-and-key and induced fit mechanisms, high substrate specificity
  • Nucleic Acids: Nucleotides (Nitrogenous base + Pentose sugar + Phosphate); Purines (Adenine A, Guanine G) and Pyrimidines (Cytosine C, Thymine T in DNA, Uracil U in RNA); Watson-Crick Double Helix model of DNA (Complementary base pairing: A=T with 2 hydrogen bonds, G≡C with 3 hydrogen bonds); Biological functions: Replication and Protein synthesis (Transcription & Translation)
  • Vitamins: Classification into Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) and Water-soluble (B-complex, C); Specific deficiency diseases (Scurvy from Vitamin C, Rickets from Vitamin D, Beri-beri from Vitamin B1)

📖 Key Terms Glossary

Quick reference for important science vocabulary across all classes.

Sublimation
Direct solid→gas conversion without passing through liquid state. Eg: camphor, iodine, dry ice (CO₂).
Latent Heat
Heat absorbed/released during phase change at constant temperature. Fusion (solid→liquid) and Vaporisation (liquid→gas).
Tyndall Effect
Scattering of a light beam by colloidal particles, making path of light visible. Not seen in true solutions.
Diffusion
Spontaneous movement of particles from high to low concentration region. Rate: gas > liquid > solid.
Mole
Amount of substance containing 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro's number). 1 mole of gas at STP = 22.4 L.
Hybridisation
Mixing of atomic orbitals to form new equivalent hybrid orbitals. sp = linear, sp² = trigonal planar, sp³ = tetrahedral.
Le Chatelier's Principle
When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to counteract the disturbance and restore equilibrium.
Activation Energy
Minimum energy required for a chemical reaction to proceed. Catalysts lower Ea without being consumed.
Nernst Equation
E = E° - (0.0592/n)log Q. Gives cell potential at non-standard conditions. At equilibrium, E = 0.
Osmosis
Movement of solvent through semi-permeable membrane from low to high solute concentration. Osmotic pressure π = MRT.
Gibbs Free Energy
ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. ΔG <0 → spontaneous. ΔG = 0 → equilibrium. ΔG >0 → non-spontaneous.
Colligative Properties
Properties depending on number (not nature) of solute particles: vapour pressure lowering, BP elevation, FP depression, osmotic pressure.
Faraday's Laws
1st: mass ∝ charge. 2nd: same charge → mass ∝ equivalent mass. F = 96485 C/mol of electrons.
Buffer Solution
Resists pH change. Acidic buffer = weak acid + its salt (eg. CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa). pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).
van't Hoff Factor (i)
Ratio of actual particles to formula units. For non-electrolytes i=1; for electrolytes i>1 (dissociation).
Crystal Field Theory
Explains splitting of d-orbitals in coordination compounds. Strong field ligands → large Δ, low spin, more diamagnetic.
