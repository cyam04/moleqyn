Practice Rules & Quiz
Hund's Rule & Pauli Exclusion Principle
Learn the rule first, then solve board/entrance quiz questions below.

Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity

Degenerate orbitals are occupied singly with parallel spins first, then pairing starts. Spin multiplicity = 2S + 1.
p⁴ → ↑↓   ↑   ↑   → 2 unpaired electrons
↑↓
↑
↑
p² ↑   ↑   _ → 2 unpaired
p⁴ ↑↓   ↑   ↑ → 2 unpaired
d⁴ ↑   ↑   ↑   ↑   _ → 4 unpaired
d⁷ ↑↓   ↑↓   ↑   ↑   ↑ → 3 unpaired
Worked Problem: A free Fe atom (Z = 26) has 3d⁶ 4s². How many unpaired electrons?
Answer: 4. Five 3d orbitals fill singly first (5 ↑); the 6th electron pairs in one orbital (↑↓ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑), leaving 4 unpaired electrons.

Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in the same atom can have the same complete set of four quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms).
One orbital holds maximum 2 electrons with opposite spins (+½ and −½)
Allowed (2,1,0,+½) & (2,1,0,−½)
Forbidden (2,1,0,+½) & (2,1,0,+½)
Subshell capacity 2(2l+1): s=2, p=6, d=10, f=14
Shell capacity 2n² electrons maximum
Worked Problem: Maximum electrons possible in the n = 3 shell?
Answer: 18. 2n² = 2(3)² = 18 = 3s² + 3p⁶ + 3d¹⁰.
